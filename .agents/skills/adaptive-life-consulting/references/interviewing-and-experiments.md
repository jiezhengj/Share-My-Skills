# Interviewing and Real-World Experiments

## Related Documents

## 1. Interview Goal & Upfront Alignment

The interview is not a personality inventory.

Its purpose is to deeply understand the user's real-world context, deconstruct execution friction, test working hypotheses, and collaboratively arrive at sound beliefs and actions.

### Upfront Alignment on Depth and Budget
During the initial turn or intake phase, calibrate the user's intended depth:
- **Quick Direction (~3–6 turns)**: The user wants concise scoping, directional steering, or high-level candidate recommendations;
- **Deep Exploration (~6–12+ turns)**: The user seeks multi-turn exploratory deconstruction of behavioral patterns, psychological friction, life context, trade-offs, and multi-angle hypothesis testing;
- Establish the user's time and round expectations to pace the consultation appropriately.

---

## 2. Question Admission Gate

Before asking:

1. Is the target variable genuinely unknown?
2. Is the user the best positioned source to provide experiential or contextual evidence?
3. Does the inquiry focus on a single, atomic dimension?
4. Is it free of compound multi-part questions?
5. Is the inquiry decision-relevant rather than unrelated trivia?
6. Would RESEARCH, INSPECT, or TEST produce more reliable evidence than asking the user?
7. Is there a higher-value latent external dependency that should be investigated now?

If the answer fails the gate, do not ask.

The atomic-question rule limits only information requested from the user. It does not prohibit external research, record inspection, analysis, or reporting findings in the same turn.

When several questions pass the gate, prefer the one with the greatest expected effect on the next action. Depth calibration is not automatically first; omit or infer it when the user has already signaled the desired depth. Do not access private calendars, purchases, health records, or location histories unless the user has supplied them for the case or explicitly authorized inspection.

---

## 3. Decision Sensitivity

Ask internally:

> If this variable were A rather than B, how much would the recommended action or strategy change?

High-sensitivity variables deserve earlier investigation.

Low-sensitivity variables may be deferred to provider selection or implementation.

---

## 4. Atomic Single-Question Cadence and Indirect Probing

### Atomic Single-Question Cadence
Default strictly to asking ONE atomic question per turn:
- Makes adaptation easier;
- Reduces cognitive fatigue and anchoring;
- Lets the user answer naturally and thoroughly;
- **Strict Prohibition**: Never link multiple inquiry dimensions into compound questions using conjunctions ("meanwhile / also / additionally / besides / 同时 / 另外 / 顺便 / 以及").

### Indirect Probing and Triangulation
- Never treat a user's single choice as a mechanical branch switch that immediately triggers a recommendation;
- Treat statements as evidentiary clues to formulate working hypotheses regarding underlying mechanisms (e.g. external accountability vs self-direction; tolerance for slow-burn worldbuilding vs demand for immediate immersion);
- Use external research to enrich understanding and break stereotypes;
- Design subsequent indirect inquiries or scenario questions to cross-verify and triangulate the working model before presenting solutions.

---

## 5. "I Don't Know" Routing

### Memory unknown

"I don't remember."

Usually stop reconstructing weak memory.

### Prediction unknown

"I've never tried it, so I don't know."

Use TEST if important.

### External fact unknown

"I don't know how much it costs."

Use RESEARCH.

### Problem-definition unknown

"I don't know what I want."

Further exploration may be appropriate.

### Conditional unknown

"It depends."

Investigate the condition if it changes action.

Repeated inability to answer the same kind of predictive question is a signal to stop introspection.

## 6. Interaction Budgets & Interleaved Evidence Progression

Budgets provide structured pacing and exploration depth:

### Quick Direction (~3–6 substantive turns)
For narrow, reversible, low-stakes problems, or when the user explicitly requests rapid steering.

### Standard Consultation (~6–12 substantive turns)
For multi-factor life, habit, hobby, and everyday-life decisions.

### Deep Consultation (~12–20+ substantive turns / 30–60+ min window)
For major career transitions, complex life dilemmas, or deep behavioral reorganization.

### Interleaved Progression

Do not reserve external research for a later phase. From intake onward:

1. Map the problem, the person, the environment, real supply, domain mechanisms, and the path to execution.
2. Run ASK / INSPECT for private and behavioral evidence while executing RESEARCH for external facts, option discovery, and mechanism correction.
3. Let findings from either track change the next question, search, hypothesis, or candidate pool.
4. Route non-introspectable uncertainty to a safe TEST.
5. Converge only after decisive external dependencies are verified or explicitly left conditional and the next action is executable.


## 7. Decision-World and Dependency Discovery

Before prolonged inquiry or option ranking, inspect six dimensions:

- **Problem**: success condition and decision horizon;
- **Person**: private goals, experiences, behavior, health, resources, and constraints;
- **Environment**: physical, social, legal, institutional, and temporal context;
- **Supply**: options and providers that actually exist and are accessible;
- **Mechanism**: how the domain works in practice, including common failure modes;
- **Execution**: the concrete path from recommendation to a sustainable action.

Generate latent unknowns even when the user has not named them. Examples include jurisdiction, travel origin, schedule, provider format, current eligibility, professional safety constraints, and whether a theoretically suitable option is locally obtainable.

Ask for the minimum private coordinate that unlocks material research. Location may range from country to transit stop or travel-time radius; omit it entirely when geography cannot change the decision.

If a coordinate is missing, ask one atomic question and continue any useful coordinate-independent research in the same turn or before the next answer.

## 8. Checkpoint Triggers

Run a checkpoint:

- after roughly 5–8 substantive answers;
- after a major hard constraint;
- after a major hypothesis reversal;
- before broadening scope;
- before exceeding budget;
- when low-value profiling starts;
- when resuming;
- before closing a complex case.

Checkpoint questions:

1. What problem are we solving now?
2. Has the framing changed?
3. What counts as success?
4. What facts are decisive?
5. Which hypotheses strengthened?
6. Which failed?
7. What are the top remaining unknowns?
8. Which has the highest decision sensitivity?
9. Which evidence route is best next?
10. If we stopped now, what would we recommend?
11. Could another question realistically change that?
12. Is action now more informative?

---

## 9. Avoid Personality-Test Logic and Attribute Mapping

Do not map a trait label directly to a recommendation, and do not silently project object attributes into user motivations.

When a user mentions a concrete past favorite or experience (an Anchor):
- Treat it strictly as an unexamined clue;
- Probe for the specific slice that worked (e.g. "What specific aspect or moment actually resonated?") or what failed;
- Never assume the user likes the object for its conventional genre, domain, or marketing attributes.

Investigate:
- actual behavior;
- actual constraints;
- latent tensions and hesitation points (e.g. fatigue vs ambition, high standards vs limited bandwidth);
- reward structure;
- context;
- environment;
- historical persistence;
- failure modes.

---

## 10. Behavior Over Trait Labels

Prefer:

> What happened the last three times?

over:

> Are you disciplined?

Prefer:

> What changed when school/work/community disappeared?

over:

> Are you intrinsically motivated?

Use behavior to generate hypotheses, not permanent labels.

---

## 11. Preference vs Tolerance

Ask or infer separately:

- can tolerate;
- prefers;
- can sustain.

Do not treat them as equivalent.

---

## 12. Real-World Experiment Trigger

Design an experiment when:

- the user cannot reliably predict;
- the unknown matters;
- a safe and reversible test exists;
- the test can discriminate among plausible hypotheses;
- observation has higher information value than more dialogue.

---

## 13. Experiment Structure

A good experiment includes:

### What to do

Use a realistic version of the long-term experience.

### What not to substitute

Avoid proxies that test the wrong variable.

### What to observe

Possible dimensions:

- pre-action reluctance;
- time perception;
- attention;
- desire to retry after failure;
- spontaneous curiosity afterward;
- physical or emotional after-effects;
- behavior 24–48 hours later;
- willingness to repeat after novelty fades.

### What to ask other people

- actual progression;
- cost;
- schedule;
- attrition;
- hidden expectations;
- eligibility;
- typical beginner timeline.

### What result updates the model

Specify which hypotheses or conclusions would change.

---

## 14. Novelty Control

First experiences may be unusually positive or negative.

When novelty is a major confound:

- repeat the promising option;
- compare the second experience;
- observe whether motivation persists once the route and procedure are familiar.

---

## 15. Reversibility

Prefer experiments that are:

- low-cost;
- reversible;
- information-rich.

Examples:

- realistic full session before annual membership;
- short project before degree enrollment;
- short-term stay before relocation;
- informational interview before resignation;
- one-week workflow before rebuilding a whole system.

---

## 16. Natural Convergence and Anti-Looping Rules

### Natural Convergence Gate
Before presenting concrete candidate recommendations, structured strategies, or action plans:
1. **Context and friction deconstructed**: The real-world dilemma, past failure points, and energy/time bandwidth are explicitly identified;
2. **Mechanisms and hypotheses verified**: Reasons for past likes/dislikes and working models have been corroborated through user evidence and indirect probing, not assumed from labels;
3. **Candidate options collided and refined**: External research has expanded candidate space (checking recent releases, diverse options, and verified pitfalls) and candidate nuances have been tested against user reactions.

### Anti-Looping Rule
Stop expanding inquiries and guide naturally to a tailored conclusion when:
- Critical variables and user preferences have stabilized across consecutive turns;
- The candidate path has achieved genuine user resonance and remaining friction is resolved;
- Further questions would merely generate redundant conversation without new contextual variables.

A great consultation often concludes with a high-resonance action plan and a lightweight, low-pressure experiment.
